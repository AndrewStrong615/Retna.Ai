What this is, and is not

Scan only: every colour and every height comes from the data, the curve does not. Colours are the photo's own pixels (or the OCT's grey values). Inside the scanned square the surface rises by the measured retina thickness, and the cut edges show the OCT's own cross-sections with its real layers. NOT measured: the curve itself (a ball of radius 11 mm, where the retina lies in a standard 24 mm eye; the OCT's own tilt is dropped), where the square sits on it, and any height OUTSIDE the square: there the photo is laid at the scan's median thickness and its rim is plain grey, because one photo holds no depth. "Light the relief" adds shading so the shape is easier to see; it darkens colours, so it is off by default here.
"Guess relief from photo" is an estimate, not a measurement. It raises thin dark lines (vessels) and lowers the brightest area (the optic disc) of the photo, from brightness alone. Real vessels are not that much raised and a bright disc is not always a deep one. Use it to look, never to measure; clicks keep reporting only the OCT's numbers.
Textbook eye: the eye is a drawing, the patch is measured. Sclera, choroid, cornea, iris, ciliary body, zonules, lens, muscles and optic nerve are a standard 24 mm eye drawn for orientation, the same for every scan; the coats of the wall are drawn thicker than life so their colours can be seen. NOT measured: this eye's size, exactly where the scan sits on the back wall (assumed: centred on the back pole) and the side of the optic nerve (choose it above; with a photo, the real one shows as the bright disc). Measured: the retina's thickness everywhere in the patch, and where the photo lines up with the scan. The scan's own tilt is dropped here: its lower surface lies on the wall and the inner surface rises by the measured thickness.
Relief. At 1x the patch has its true proportions (a retina is about 0.3 mm thick on an 11 mm wall, so swelling is hard to see). Up to 5x multiplies only the thickness, of the patch and of the drawn retina around it alike, so the patch stays flush and differences stand out. The numbers never change with it. With "Cut away in front" on, the part of the patch in front of the B-scan is removed; the dark red floor there is the layer under the retina (the scan's lower surface, laid on the wall).
Flat block shape: exactly as scanned. Every point of the surface comes from the OCT scan, in micrometres. "Relief" only makes it easier to see; at 1x the proportions are true.
The surfaces are found by a classical method (strongest dark-to-bright edges, joined into smooth lines), not by a trained model. It can fail in shadows, very swollen retina, or at the optic disc. Check the lines on the B-scan. The lower line is the top of the bright band, so thickness reads some tens of micrometres lower than a device's own number.
Red = thicker than the threshold, which by default means clearly thicker than the rest of this scan. There is no database of normal eyes behind it. It is a measurement, not a diagnosis.
No AI heat map here. The disease model reads fundus photos and has never seen OCT. Give oct3d.py a photo of the same eye (--photo, --heat) to lay its findings onto this surface.
