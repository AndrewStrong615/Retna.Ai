What this is, and is not

Eyeball shape: the globe is a drawing, the patch is measured. The eyeball is a standard 24 mm one with cornea, lens, iris and optic nerve drawn for orientation. NOT measured: this eye's size, exactly where the scan sits on the back wall (assumed: centred on the back pole) and the side of the optic nerve (choose it above; with a photo, the real one shows as the bright disc). Measured: the retina's thickness everywhere in the patch, and where the photo lines up with the scan. The scan's own tilt is dropped here: its lower surface lies on the wall and the inner surface rises by the measured thickness.
Flat block shape: exactly as scanned. Every point of the surface comes from the OCT scan, in micrometres. "Stretch the depth" only makes the relief easier to see; at 1x the proportions are true.
The surfaces are found by a classical method (strongest dark-to-bright edges, joined into smooth lines), not by a trained model. It can fail in shadows, very swollen retina, or at the optic disc. Check the lines on the B-scan. The lower line is the top of the bright band, so thickness reads some tens of micrometres lower than a device's own number.
Red = thicker than the threshold, which by default means clearly thicker than the rest of this scan. There is no database of normal eyes behind it. It is a measurement, not a diagnosis.
No AI heat map here. The disease model reads fundus photos and has never seen OCT. Give oct3d.py a photo of the same eye (--photo, --heat) to lay its findings onto this surface.
